// DISSERTATION  ·  IN PROGRESS
AESTHETIC EXPERIENCE AS EXPLANANDUM: A NEUROPHENOMENOLOGICAL ACCOUNT OF BEING MOVED ONGOING
PhD thesis. Argues that aesthetic experience is radically decentered—constitutively relational and hetero-referential. Combines micro-phenomenological interviews with EEG microstate analysis. 22 participants, 113 artworks, 64 channels.

Theoretical backbone: Figal's phenomenology of appearing, Arsiwalla's integrated information framework (methodological borrowing only). Structured following Elliott (2021): prototype, demonstration, post facto argumentation.
